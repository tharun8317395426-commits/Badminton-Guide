About Badminton

Badminton is one of the fastest racket sports in the world, played with lightweight rackets and a feathered or synthetic shuttlecock across a high central net. The modern game originated in mid-19th century British India as a variant of the earlier game battledore and shuttlecock, and was later formalized at Badminton House in Gloucestershire, England.

Today, badminton is an Olympic sport governed by the Badminton World Federation (BWF). It enjoys immense popularity across Asia and Europe, especially in countries like India, China, Indonesia, Malaysia, Japan, and Denmark, drawing millions of passionate amateur and professional players.

Why Play Badminton?

  • Excellent cardiovascular workout that burns up to 450 calories per hour.
  • Develops lightning-fast reflexes, hand-eye coordination, and mental agility.
  • Strengthens leg muscles, core stability, and upper body flexibility.
  • Fun social sport that can be played in both singles and doubles formats.
  • Helps reduce daily stress while improving concentration and endurance.

Game at a Glance

Feature Standard Specification
Players 1 vs 1 (Singles) or 2 vs 2 (Doubles)
Points to win a game 21 points (must lead by at least 2 points; cap at 30)
Games in a match Best of 3 games
Court type Indoor wooden or synthetic mat court
Shuttle material Feather (goose/duck feathers) or synthetic nylon
